# SKUbase pricing assessment — 2026-09-06

## Recommendation

Keep $29 Starter and $99 Growth as the published targets while validating paid
conversion. $199 Scale is a plausible market price, but its operational promises
need to be proven before actively selling it to large, multi-location stores.
Installation reliability and trustworthy imported data matter more than a price
cut at this stage. Do not change existing subscriptions or the price-lock promise
as part of this assessment.

## Assessment by tier

**Starter — $29:** Reasonable for a merchant who receives useful ranked actions,
stock alerts, and dead-stock guidance. It is not the cheapest option. Include an
obvious usable output in the trial and retain basic inventory CSV export so a
small merchant can act on the analysis. The jump to $99 must buy a complete
planning workflow rather than access to basic data.

**Growth — $99:** Defensible once forecasts, reorder planning, and exports work
reliably on real stores. Fabrikatör starts at the same price and Prediko starts
lower for small merchants, so compete on clarity, SKU-based billing, and time
saved. Demonstrate the path from stockout risk to a reviewable purchase plan.
Do not lower the permanent list price before measuring trial-to-paid conversion
and recording why merchants decline.

**Scale — $199:** The price itself is realistic; delivery and cost are the risks.
The current Shopify sync stores aggregate inventory, while the tier advertises
10-location capacity and transfer planning conditional on location data. Sync is
still synchronous. Prove location-level ingestion and large-catalog performance
before positioning this as a ready replacement for operational inventory tools.
Same-business-day support and concierge onboarding need a sustainable staffing
budget. A guided pilot is more appropriate until those checks pass.

## Presentation and promise issues

The September 6 repair pass corrects the annual equivalents, removes unsupported
popularity and competitor-price claims, removes the unimplemented grace-period
notification promise, and explains read-only and aggregate-inventory limits.
Listed billing amounts and existing rate commitments are unchanged. The points
below record the audit findings and remaining implementation checks.

- Replace the blanket claim that the rest of the market hides pricing: Assisty,
  Prediko, and Fabrikatör all publish prices. Emphasize SKUbase's specific pricing
  model and predictability instead.
- Use a neutral label such as Recommended for Growth unless real subscription
  data supports Most merchants pick this.
- Annual display arithmetic differs slightly from billing: $296/year equals
  $24.67/month, $1,010 equals $84.17, and $2,030 equals $169.17. The current display
  showed $24.65/$84.15/$169.15. The repaired display uses the rounded equivalents
  above, exact annual totals, and approximately 15% savings.
- Make the SKU definition and over-limit workflow explicit. The page promises an
  email, banner, and 30-day grace period; verify the implementation before relying
  on that promise. A code search did not establish SKU/location/seat enforcement.
  The unverified email/banner/grace-period promise has been removed; enforcement
  and the actual over-limit handling still need a deliberate product decision.
- Separate read-only recommendations from Shopify inventory execution. Imported
  aggregate stock does not establish location-aware receiving or transfers.
- Preserve existing rate commitments. Any future packaging changes should be
  reviewed separately from existing merchant subscriptions.

## Validation targets

Interview the first 5–10 activated merchants. Record time to first successful
sync, time to a useful action, trial conversion, weekly repeated use, support
minutes per account, and reasons for cancellation. For illustration only, one
hour saved each month valued by a merchant at $30 covers Starter; Growth and
Scale need greater recurring value. Cost savings must be demonstrated, not
promised from hypothetical forecasts.

Measure hosting, data processing, support, and acquisition costs before making
claims about profitability. The market comparison establishes plausibility, not
willingness to pay or unit economics.
