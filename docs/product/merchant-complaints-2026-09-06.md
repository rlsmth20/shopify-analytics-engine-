# SKUbase: merchant complaints and the next product priorities

Research date: September 6, 2026. Research only; no application changes.

## Recommendation

Win the first paying customers with a reliable weekly buying workflow: import the
store accurately, explain the reorder recommendation, let the merchant adjust it,
and export a supplier-ready plan. Prove that loop before presenting SKUbase as a
complete Stocky replacement or selling broad multi-location operations.

This is a product recommendation inferred from the evidence below. The opportunity
is execution and a clearly bounded promise. Competitors already deliver useful
forecasts, purchase orders, migration help, and responsive support to many stores.
A lower price or more dashboard pages alone is unlikely to displace them.

## Evidence and limits

This is a purposive sample of Shopify App Store merchant reviews for Inventory
Planner by Sage, Prediko, Fabrikatör, Assisty, Sumtracker, and Stockie. It emphasizes
low-rating feedback and checks recent positive reviews for counterevidence. It is
not a census, a failure-rate estimate, or proof that any reported defect remains
unfixed. Shopify Magic summaries and competitor comparison marketing were not
used as merchant testimony. Dates below are the displayed review dates; edited
dates are explicitly labeled. Filtered pages sometimes return differently cached
rating totals, so this report does not compare complaint percentages.

### 1. Sync failure makes the whole subscription unusable — repeated within one app

Morning Lavender, October 20, 2025, reported a prolonged failed connection after
annual payment. Altenew, January 28, 2025, separately reported intermittent sync
and stale data. Inventory Planner's February 14 reply to Altenew said a fix had
been deployed. These support the importance of freshness and recovery, not a claim
that Inventory Planner is currently broken. [Morning Lavender review](https://apps.shopify.com/inventory-planner/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1),
[Altenew review and response](https://apps.shopify.com/inventory-planner/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=2).

**SKUbase implication:** sync health is part of the product's value. A plausible
recommendation on stale or incomplete data can be worse than an explicit pause.

### 2. Merchants misunderstand which system controls stock — repeated, disputed cases

A Twist of Yarn, March 10, 2026, reported POS inventory adjustments being reversed.
Sumtracker replied that its master-inventory sync can overwrite outside changes
and that setup contains warnings. Timeless Sneaker Boutique, November 20, 2025,
reported inventory loss; the developer explained that shared SKUs update together
and offered restoration. These are two distinct onboarding/stock-control cases,
not independent proof of data corruption. [Reviews and developer responses](https://apps.shopify.com/sumtracker-fulfil-ship-track/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1).

**SKUbase implication:** preserve the read-only starting experience, validate
duplicate and missing SKU mappings, and make any future stock-writing mode a
separate, understandable action with a preview and history.

### 3. Complexity and slow screens obstruct daily buying — current signal with older corroboration

Assisty merchant eturelmadrid, August 4, 2026, described duplicate information,
difficult navigation, slow actions, and unanswered complex requests. In its
August 5 Spanish-language response, Assisty agreed that simplification and speed
needed improvement. TA3's December 21, 2024 Inventory Planner review also described
navigation and save delays. The first summary is translated from Spanish; the
merchant's claim that acquaintances agree is not counted as additional reviews.
[Assisty review](https://apps.shopify.com/assisty/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1),
[TA3 review](https://apps.shopify.com/reviews/1638835).

**SKUbase implication:** prioritize a fast purchase decision table with useful
filters and saved views before adding more analytical destinations.

### 4. Generic forecasts create manual work — supported historical signal

Wild Oak Boutique, August 14, 2024, reported manual overrides for seasonal versus
new fast-selling products and difficulty accounting for stockouts. TA3, December
21, 2024, reported seasonal overforecasting. AMQUA's positive April 21, 2025 review
described limited Shopify data skewing forecasts, with support resolving the
situation. These establish messy-data use cases, not verified current feature
limitations across the market. [Wild Oak Boutique](https://apps.shopify.com/reviews/1498939),
[TA3](https://apps.shopify.com/reviews/1638835),
[AMQUA](https://apps.shopify.com/inventory-planner/reviews).

**SKUbase implication:** show history coverage, stockout observations, method,
assumptions, and editable inputs. Do not present inferred historical stockouts as
observed facts. Separate new-product and seasonal handling.

### 5. Promised fit must be demonstrated during evaluation — distinct one-off cases

Prediko's Wild Junkie review, edited May 4, 2026, reports unresolved bundle
prediction needs. Its displayed developer response is dated December 3, 2025,
before that edit. Novaa Gear, February 23, 2025, expected production availability
after importing materials; Prediko acknowledged import friction but disputed
advertising made-to-order functionality. These are different requirements, not
two reports of the same bug. [Prediko reviews and responses](https://apps.shopify.com/prediko/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1).

Nuvibody's Fabrikatör review, edited May 13, 2026, reports a preorder workflow that
still failed after extended support. This is one merchant's report, not evidence
that Fabrikatör preorders generally fail. [Nuvibody review](https://apps.shopify.com/fabrikator/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1).

**SKUbase implication:** identify bundle, preorder, manufacturing, and location
requirements before the trial. Demonstrate the merchant's actual case or state
the unsupported requirement early.

### 6. Pricing and contract friction motivate switching — repeated, concentrated evidence

Inventory Planner's George and Willy, February 11, 2024, and Beauti, February 23,
2025, described higher renewal proposals/fees. Modkat, July 9, 2025, reported an
annual contract it could no longer use after a 3PL change; the developer said it
offered custom-integration/CSV options. These support clearer terms and fit
qualification, not a blanket assertion that competing prices are hidden.
[George and Willy / Modkat](https://apps.shopify.com/inventory-planner/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1),
[Beauti and response](https://apps.shopify.com/inventory-planner/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=2).

Fabrikatör merchant Trooper Beer EU, April 22, 2025, objected to backorder usage
charges; the developer defended published, equal pricing. This is an isolated
budget objection, not evidence of hidden billing. [Review and response](https://apps.shopify.com/fabrikator/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1).

**SKUbase implication:** explain active SKU counting, excluded products, over-limit
handling, billing frequency, and cancellation before checkout. Retain existing
price commitments; do not lower permanent prices on review anecdotes alone.

### 7. The competitive bar is already high

Stockie's Loco Skates review, August 6, 2026, praises stockout-day handling,
supplier-specific restock periods, and sales columns inside purchase orders.
The Healthy Place, August 31, praises migration help, receiving, and cost
management. [Stockie merchant reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/stockie/reviews).

Stockie's Panda Hobby, July 28, 2026, questioned its replacement value after a
displayed five minutes of use; the developer disputed the characterization and
said transfers were then in development. This is a weak, one-off signal about
expectations, not evidence the app is merely a reskin. [Review and response](https://apps.shopify.com/stockie/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1).

Fabrikatör's Plümo Ltd, June 24, 2026, praises straightforward PO creation and
receiving. Sumtracker's Continental Wine & Spirits, April 21, 2026, values average
costing and PO receiving. [Plümo Ltd](https://apps.shopify.com/fabrikator/reviews),
[Continental Wine & Spirits](https://apps.shopify.com/sumtracker-fulfil-ship-track/reviews).

**SKUbase implication:** simple buying, helpful support, and a Stocky migration
story are competitive requirements. Do not claim they are unserved or unique.

## What should ship next

These are proposed acceptance criteria, not claims about currently delivered
functionality. A follow-up source check found that the current purchase-order
page and `purchase_order_records.py` already persist editable drafts, status
changes, partial receipts, and supplier observations. The buying calendar shows
remaining incoming quantities. These capabilities need end-to-end verification,
not rebuilding based on the older May gap analysis. Identified follow-up checks
include duplicate/over-quantity receipt handling, netting open orders against new
recommendations, and supplier lead-time calculations (the current observation
loader assumes a 14-day baseline). Location inventory is still aggregate during
Shopify sync. The [pricing assessment](pricing-assessment-2026-09-06.md) records
the resulting commercial limits.

| Priority | Deliverable | Evidence it is ready |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | Dependable first import and ongoing sync | Merchant can see stages, last successful sync, imported history, record counts, failures and recovery. No successful-looking empty report after failure; reconcile sample products with Shopify. |
| P0 | One reviewable buying plan | From an at-risk SKU, see available stock, incoming units where known, lead time, demand basis and proposed quantity; edit assumptions, apply supplier minimums/case sizes where configured, and export a supplier-ready draft. Label unknown incoming stock explicitly. |
| P0 | Forecast explanations and data limits | Sparse/new/seasonal cases show distinct rationale; stale data and missing history remain visible; genuine stockout history is distinguished from estimates. Evaluate recommendations against held-out sales and merchant judgment before promising savings. |
| P1 | Fast daily workspace | Search, supplier/risk filters, saved columns and bulk selection operate on the supported catalog size; one page covers review, adjustment and export. Set and measure a response-time budget on real pilot catalogs. |
| P1 | Verify and harden the existing purchasing loop | Exercise persisted open orders, expected dates, partial receipts, cancellations and remaining quantities; prevent duplicate/over-quantity receipts; confirm incoming stock reduces new recommendations. Validate actual supplier lead times before operational replacement claims. |
| P1 | Reliable fit checks and support | Trial setup records required workflows, unsupported needs, and an owner for blockers. Measure resolution and onboarding time; publish only support response commitments that can be staffed. |
| Later, after validation | Location operations, bundles and supplier scoring | Use real location demand/component mappings/receipt observations. Pilot separately; do not fill missing operational data with persuasive-looking scores. |

## Acquisition and packaging implications

1. **Initial customer hypothesis:** Shopify merchants with one principal stocking
   location who already buy through supplier spreadsheets and can act on an
   exported plan. They can evaluate planning value without waiting for a full
   inventory execution system. Validate this segment with actual trials.
2. **Positioning to test:** "Know what to reorder, when, and why — with a buying
   plan you can check and predictable SKU-based pricing." Use only when the
   demonstrated product and billing implementation support each promise.
3. **Show the result:** a short real-store demonstration from import health to
   adjusted supplier plan. Use anonymized, permissioned examples and measured
   time saved; do not advertise hypothetical cash savings as results.
4. **Offer assisted activation within a small pilot:** validate one real reorder
   cycle with each merchant. Record time to first usable plan, repeat weekly use,
   exported plans, payment decisions, support time, and exact reasons for refusal.
   The first 5–10 merchants are qualitative learning, not market-wide validation.
5. **Keep $29/$99 as targets while testing value.** The $99 tier needs repeated
   buying-workflow value. Keep $199 operational promises in a guided pilot until
   location and purchasing capabilities are proven. Review complaints establish
   switching motives; they do not establish willingness to pay SKUbase.
6. **Use accurate comparison content:** publish a fit checklist and supported
   migration fields, with a working sample import. Avoid named attack pages built
   around a handful of negative reviews. Build Stocky replacement acquisition
   only around workflows SKUbase can actually complete.

The near-term decision is whether merchants repeatedly use and pay for the buying
plan. Broad multi-channel synchronization, manufacturing, preorders and extra
analytics should follow demonstrated demand and reliable operational data.
