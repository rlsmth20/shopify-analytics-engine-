## First-contact writing rules

Lead with what Skubase does and the practical benefit to this merchant. Aim for
90–130 words, shorter when the channel calls for it. Include:

1. Obvious promotional disclosure: "Skubase promotion:" and identify the app.
2. One verified merchant fact or their actual public question.
3. One or two relevant, verified capabilities and the benefit to their workflow.
4. The owner-requested disclosure: "Skubase is currently in Shopify's review
   process and is not yet listed in the Shopify App Store."
5. One concrete question OR one low-friction offer, such as a free five-product
   inventory health check. Do not stack several calls to action.

Do not routinely append defensive disclaimers: "I can't promise", "without
promising a feature or delivery date", "it cannot predict every sudden demand
spike", "no purchase obligation", or "ongoing subscriptions are separate".
A free health check can simply be called a free health check. Explain actual
pricing or limitations when the merchant asks or when necessary to make a
specific claim accurate. Do not imply the paid app itself is free.

Do not frame unknown capability as an absent capability. Consult the verified
facts below or the exact relevant implementation before declaring a feature gap.
If something is still unknown, omit that claim from introductory copy and retain
an internal question. Do not turn internal uncertainty into a paragraph of doubt
in an advertisement.

Offer collaboration only when the merchant's requested workflow has a real,
specific gap: "We can work with you to understand the workflow and explore a
practical solution." It is not a mandatory footer. Do not guarantee unbuilt work,
custom development or delivery dates; avoiding those promises does not require
announcing that you cannot promise them.

## Verified product facts

Verified September 9 against the existing app code:

- Reorder priorities use recent sales, stock on hand, supplier lead times and
  safety-stock settings. They help decide what to buy and when.
- Supplier purchase-order drafts can be saved and exported as Excel (.xlsx)
  workbooks. Individual PO exports include supplier, quantities, unit costs,
  line costs, shipping and totals. Source: `frontend/lib/report-export.ts`,
  `exportPurchaseOrderReport`; UI: `frontend/app/(app-shell)/purchase-orders/page.tsx`.
- The buying plan can also be exported, with supplier and line details. Source:
  `exportBuyPlanReport` in the same report-export module.
- Stock and sales analysis flags potential slow/dead stock and excess inventory,
  helping merchants prioritize inventory cash and replenishment decisions.

Describe the verified Excel format when format matters. A claim of CSV/PDF export,
compatibility with a specific supplier system, or plan availability needs its own
verification. These are internal claim-checking rules, not copy to insert in ads.
